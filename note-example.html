Placeholder: a longer note with room to breathe

Open with the situation in plain language. What was true before you did anything? A good first paragraph gives the reader enough context to care and no more. Two to four sentences is plenty.

Then the turn: what you or the team actually did. Write it the way you'd explain it to a smart friend outside your company. If a sentence would work in a press release, rewrite it.

A section heading when the note has parts

Use headings only when the note genuinely has sections; a 300-word note usually doesn't need any. Lists work like this when you have a genuine sequence or set:

  • First concrete thing, stated as a fact rather than a virtue.
  • Second concrete thing, ideally with a number in it.
  • Third thing, and stop. Three is usually enough.
A pull quote is for the one sentence you'd want someone to remember if they remember nothing else.

Below is the screenshot pattern. Drop your image into the images/ folder, update the src, and always write the caption and alt text; the caption carries the "so what" and the alt text is what screen readers and search engines see.

Close by saying what changed because of the work, or what you'd do differently. Endings that admit something are more credible than endings that declare something.
